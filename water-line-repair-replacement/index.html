A water line problem often shows up as low pressure throughout the entire house, a wet spot in the yard along the line's path, or an unusually high water bill, all pointing to a supply-side issue rather than anything inside the home's interior plumbing.

What Causes A Water Line To Fail

Older galvanized or original copper service lines corrode over decades just like interior supply lines do. A service line failure often shows up as a wet spot in the yard between the meter and the house, sometimes well before it causes a noticeable pressure drop inside.

Ground movement, tree root pressure, or simple age-related material fatigue can also cause a service line to crack or separate at a joint. Because this line runs underground, a failure is often invisible until either a wet spot appears at the surface or the water bill spikes from continuous loss.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • Low water pressure throughout the entire house, not just one fixture.
  • A consistently wet or soggy area in the yard along the line's likely path.
  • An unexplained increase in your water bill.
  • The water meter's leak indicator moving with all fixtures off.
  • Discolored water right after the city or your utility does nearby line work.

Locating A Water Line Problem

We use the water meter test to confirm a leak exists, then acoustic listening equipment to narrow down the leak's location along the line's path before any digging starts. This avoids trenching the entire yard on a guess about where the problem actually is.

Spot Repair Versus Full Line Replacement

A single, confirmed leak point is often a straightforward spot repair once located. A line with widespread corrosion, multiple past leaks, or one that is simply undersized for the home's current demand is a better candidate for full replacement, sometimes using trenchless methods to minimize yard disruption.

Water line repair and replacement is generally not covered by homeowner's insurance, as it is treated as a maintenance item related to the property's own service line rather than a sudden covered event.

Spot Repair Vs. Full Line Replacement

Spot Repair

  • Lower cost for a single, confirmed leak point.
  • Faster to complete.
  • Does not address other aging sections of the line.
  • Appropriate for a line with one isolated failure.

Full Line Replacement

  • Addresses corrosion along the entire line.
  • Can correct an undersized line for current household demand.
  • Higher upfront cost than a single repair.
  • Recommended after repeated leaks on the same line.
