Installation of a new shower or bathtub, including the rough-in plumbing work behind an outdated or damaged fixture. Common in both bathroom remodels and straightforward fixture replacements.

A shower or tub replacement is as much a plumbing job as a cosmetic one, since the valve, drain, and supply connections behind the fixture often need attention alongside installing the new unit itself, especially in an older home.

Why This Often Involves More Than The Visible Fixture

Original shower and tub valves in Prescott's older homes are sometimes decades old, and replacing the visible fixture without addressing an aging valve behind the wall can mean revisiting the same wall again soon after. We check valve condition as part of any shower or tub installation rather than assuming it is fine simply because it still works.

A tub-to-shower conversion, increasingly common as homeowners look to update older bathrooms, also involves reworking the drain configuration and sometimes the surrounding wall framing, which is a bigger job than a straightforward like-for-like tub replacement.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • A cracked, chipped, or badly worn tub or shower surface.
  • A leaking valve behind the wall that shows up as water damage.
  • Wanting to convert an underused tub into a walk-in shower.
  • A remodel that includes updating an outdated bathroom fixture.
  • Low water pressure specifically at the shower or tub, suggesting valve issues.

What We Check Before Installation

We assess the condition of the existing valve, drain, and supply lines behind the current fixture, since these often need attention as part of an installation even when the request started as a simple fixture swap. This prevents a second visit soon after for a problem that could have been addressed during the original job.

Like-For-Like Replacement Versus A Tub-To-Shower Conversion

A like-for-like tub or shower replacement, using the existing footprint and drain location, is the more straightforward and less expensive option. Converting a tub into a shower, or vice versa, involves reworking the drain position and often the surrounding wall structure, a bigger project with a correspondingly higher cost.

Like-For-Like Vs. Tub-To-Shower Conversion

Like-For-Like Replacement

  • Lower cost using the existing footprint and drain.
  • Faster installation timeline.
  • Keeps the same fixture type as before.
  • Best when the existing configuration works for your needs.

Tub-To-Shower Conversion

  • Modernizes an underused or outdated tub.
  • Requires drain and wall reconfiguration.
  • Higher cost than a straight replacement.
  • Popular for homeowners prioritizing shower access over a tub.

Safety Steps Near A Wet Installation Area.

  • Any outlet within six feet of a shower or tub must be GFCI-protected under current code, and we verify this on every installation.
  • Do not operate a portable light or power tool near standing water during the installation process.
  • A light fixture added or relocated near the new shower or tub must be rated for damp or wet locations.
  • We test GFCI protection after the job is complete, not just visually confirm the outlet type.
