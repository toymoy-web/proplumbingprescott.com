A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from being drawn backward into the clean water supply, a real concern anywhere an irrigation system, well, or other non-potable connection ties into the same plumbing as household water.

Why This Matters More For Irrigation-Heavy Properties

Properties with significant irrigation systems, common on larger lots around Williamson Valley and the ranch properties in Chino Valley, have a real cross-connection risk if the irrigation system ties into the same water supply as the house. A backflow event could draw fertilizer, pesticide residue, or standing irrigation water backward into the household supply without a functioning prevention device in place.

Many municipal and county water systems require annual backflow device testing precisely because these devices can fail silently, with no visible symptom until an actual backflow event occurs. Regular testing confirms the device still functions before it is actually needed.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • It has been over a year since your backflow device was last tested.
  • A visible leak or dripping from the backflow device itself.
  • A new irrigation system installed without a backflow preventer.
  • A commercial property renewing an annual compliance requirement.
  • Discolored or off-tasting water after irrigation system use.

What A Backflow Test Involves

A certified test checks that the device's internal check valves and relief mechanism function correctly under test conditions, confirming it will actually prevent backward flow if a real pressure differential occurs. We provide documentation of the test results for any property that needs to show compliance.

Testing Versus Device Replacement

A device that passes testing needs nothing further until the next scheduled test. A device that fails testing needs either an internal component repair or, if it is old or damaged enough, full replacement, which we will explain clearly based on what the test actually reveals.

Skipping Testing Vs. Annual Compliance

Skipping Annual Testing

  • No immediate cost.
  • Risk of a failed device going undetected.
  • Potential compliance violation for commercial properties.
  • Real contamination risk if backflow actually occurs.

Annual Testing

  • Confirms the device will work if actually needed.
  • Meets compliance requirements where applicable.
  • Catches a failing device before it becomes a real risk.
  • Standard recommended practice for any backflow preventer.
