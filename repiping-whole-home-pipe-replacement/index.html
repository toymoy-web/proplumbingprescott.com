When A Home Has Outgrown Patch Repairs

Original galvanized supply lines in Prescott's pre-1960s homes corrode from the inside over decades. Once a home has needed more than one or two galvanized pipe repairs, the math usually favors a full repipe over continuing to patch a system that is failing as a whole, not at one isolated point.

A repipe also addresses low water pressure caused by decades of internal corrosion narrowing the pipe's interior diameter throughout the house, something no single spot repair can fix since the restriction exists everywhere the original pipe still runs.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • More than one galvanized pipe repair in the past few years.
  • Persistently low water pressure throughout the house.
  • Rust-colored water, especially after water has sat unused.
  • Visible corrosion at exposed pipe connections.
  • A home inspection flagging original, aging supply lines.

Assessing Whether A Repipe Makes Sense

We evaluate the home's current pipe material, the frequency and cost of past repairs, and current water pressure and quality before recommending a full repipe. This is a bigger project than any single repair, and we want the recommendation to reflect a genuine need, not a default upsell.

Copper Versus PEX For A Repipe

Copper is the traditional choice, durable and long-lasting, though it costs more in materials and labor given the soldering involved. PEX is flexible, faster to install since it needs fewer joints, and generally costs less overall, making it the more common choice for a modern repipe unless a homeowner specifically prefers copper's track record.

Continuing Patch Repairs Vs. A Full Repipe

Continue Patch Repairs

  • Lower cost per individual repair.
  • Leaves the rest of the aging system untouched.
  • Repeated repair costs add up over time.
  • Risk of a sudden failure between repairs remains.

Full Repipe

  • One-time fix for the whole system.
  • Restores full water pressure throughout the home.
  • Higher upfront cost than any single repair.
  • Recommended once a home has needed multiple pipe repairs.

What Not To Do In Pre-1980s Walls.

  • Homes built before the 1980s can contain asbestos in old pipe wrap, wall texture, or insulation, which should never be disturbed without proper testing first.
  • Do not sand, cut, or forcibly remove old pipe insulation or wall material in a pre-1980s home without confirming its content.
  • If a wall or ceiling material is suspected to contain asbestos, a qualified inspector should test it before any demolition begins.
  • We plan repipe access points around known or suspected hazardous materials rather than cutting into them without warning.
