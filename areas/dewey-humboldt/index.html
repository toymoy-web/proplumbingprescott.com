The Most Common Calls We Get From Dewey-Humboldt.

Septic service leads the list here, more than in any other town in our coverage area. Dewey-Humboldt only incorporated in 2004, formed by merging the two older communities of Dewey and Humboldt, and much of the town remains large-lot rural residential well outside municipal sewer service.

Two Towns, Two Different Housing Ages.

Humboldt grew up around the Iron King Mine and the Humboldt Smelter in the early 1900s, and the older homes on that side of town reflect an early-20th-century mining community. Dewey, just to the north along the Agua Fria River, stayed agricultural longer and carries a different, generally newer mix of residential construction. A plumbing call on the Humboldt side is more likely to involve original, decades-old piping than one on the Dewey side.

Why Septic Comes Up So Often Here.

At roughly 4,600 to 4,800 feet and about 15 miles east of Prescott, Dewey-Humboldt's large-lot zoning and rural character mean municipal sewer simply never reached most of the town. Regular septic tank pumping and inspection is standard maintenance here in a way it rarely is for a Prescott Valley subdivision, and we treat it as routine rather than an unusual request.

24/7 Emergency Response Across Both Dewey And Humboldt.

Because so many properties are on well water and septic, a Dewey-Humboldt call often means checking both ends of the system. That is the well or pressure tank on the supply side and the septic tank on the drainage side, rather than assuming a straightforward municipal-utility connection like a tighter in-town subdivision would have. Our 24/7 emergency line dispatches to both the Dewey and Humboldt sides of town around the clock.

Working On Original Mining-Era Homes.

Some of the older homes near the historic Humboldt Smelter Stack date back to the early mining era, and original plumbing in a house that old sometimes means galvanized supply lines or cast iron drain lines well past their practical service life. We treat a service call in this part of town as an opportunity to flag a full re-pipe if the existing system is failing in more than one spot, rather than chasing the same aging pipe from one patch repair to the next.

The Agua Fria River Valley Side.

The Dewey side of town, along the Agua Fria River, stayed agricultural well into the 2000s before the last working farm there was sold for new residential building. Newer construction on that side of town generally runs modern PEX, so the plumbing profile on the Dewey side looks more like Prescott Valley's than it does the older Humboldt side just a few miles away.

A Young Town Government With Growing Infrastructure.

Dewey-Humboldt only incorporated in 2004, later than every other town in this coverage area, and municipal infrastructure here is still less built out than in the longer-established towns nearby. That is part of why septic and private well systems remain the norm rather than the exception across most of the town.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

  • Shut off the main water or well supply valve if it is safe to reach.
  • Kill power at the breaker to any wet area of the house before touching anything else.
  • Keep the area clear until a technician arrives, especially around septic access points.
  • Call our 24/7 line and let us know whether the property is on well, septic, or both.
