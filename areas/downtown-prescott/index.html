Plumbing for the restaurants, bars, shops, and offices around Courthouse Plaza and Whiskey Row. This page is written for building owners, tenants, and managers in a historic commercial district.

What This District Is

Downtown Prescott's historic core is the Courthouse Plaza Historic District, which the National Park Service lists on the National Register of Historic Places, published in December 1978. The NPS describes it as a district of commerce, architecture, and government with Late Victorian buildings, roughly bounded by Gurley, Montezuma, Cortez, and Goodwin Streets. Whiskey Row runs along Montezuma Street on the west side of the plaza.

This is mostly a commercial and civic area rather than a residential neighborhood, which is why this page speaks to building owners, tenants, and managers. If you are looking for plumbing in a historic home, see our pages for the East Prescott and West Prescott historic districts.

Why The Buildings Look The Way They Do

According to the Sharlot Hall Museum, a fire in 1900 swept Whiskey Row and the blocks around it, and they were rebuilt in brick or stone. A building that has been rebuilt and remodeled over more than a century can hold plumbing from several periods, so we trace lines before proposing repairs rather than assuming what is behind a wall.

Commercial Plumbing For Older Masonry Buildings

Restaurants and bars put fats, oils, and food waste into drain lines. As general practice, regular cleaning costs less than an emergency call during service hours, and hydro jetting can clear buildup that a cable leaves behind. We can run a camera through a line to show what is happening before recommending anything. See drain cleaning and our commercial plumbing page for the full scope.

Offices and retail spaces have their own pattern of calls, from restroom fixtures and supply leaks to water heaters. The City's 2026 permit list says water heater replacement needs a permit for commercial work as well as residential, so we plan for that on every replacement.

Permits, The Preservation Commission, And Your Building

The City's Preservation Commission reviews exterior work that requires a building permit in local preservation districts, and the City says minor maintenance and repair are not typically reviewed. We have not found a City statement showing whether a particular building on the plaza sits in a local district, so ask the City's preservation staff before work that touches the outside of a building. The City's 2026 permit list also shows water heater replacement and water or sewer line replacement as permit work.

Neighboring Historic Districts

The South Prescott Townsite, a National Register district of 15 blocks and 192 parcels, adjoins the plaza area to the south, according to the City. It mixes older commercial and residential buildings. We serve it with the same approach, and you are welcome to call to ask about your address.

Before We Arrive: A Backed-Up Drain In A Business

  • Stop using the affected sinks, floor drains, and restrooms and tell staff why.
  • Keep customers, staff, and food away from any standing wastewater.
  • If you manage the building, find out whether the problem is limited to one tenant space or affects several.
  • Call us and describe which fixtures are affected so we can bring the right equipment.
