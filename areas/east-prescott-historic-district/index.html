About The East Prescott Historic District

The East Prescott Historic District is a National Register district that the City of Prescott describes as covering portions of 23 blocks and 293 parcels. Its boundaries are the railroad right-of-way north of Sheldon Street, the alley east of Mount Vernon Street, Aubrey Street on the south, and a line that bisects the block between Marina and Alarcon Streets on the west. The City counts 187 contributing buildings, built between 1885 and 1937, with Queen Anne Victorian the most common style alongside Bungalow, Colonial Revival, and Period Revival homes.

Local District Versus National Register District

Only part of this district is also a local district. The southern half, from Gurley Street to Carleton Street, has been the Southeast Prescott Historic Preservation District since 1995, and the City's Preservation Commission reviews projects there that require a building permit. Properties in the National Register district outside that local portion are not subject to Commission project review, though the Commission's input is encouraged.

The City's general guidance says the review applies to exterior work and that minor maintenance, repair, and painting are not typically reviewed. Most plumbing happens inside the walls. The jobs worth checking with the City first are the ones that change the outside of a house, such as a new vent through a wall or roof or an exposed line.

Permits are a separate matter. The City's 2026 list of what needs a permit says water heater replacement needs a permit and that replacing a fixture does not. It also lists moving or replacing a water or sewer line as permit work, and the same list shows a permit applies when that work is in the public right-of-way.

Service Lines And Sewers On Older Blocks

Houses here date from 1885 to 1937, and the lines connecting them to the City's mains have had a long time to be repaired, replaced, and rerouted. Where your line runs, what it is made of, and what condition it is in are things we find out with a camera and a locator rather than assume. If a camera shows damage, we walk through sewer line repair and sewer line replacement and explain which one the evidence points to.

Hidden Leaks And Unexplained Water Bills

If your bill climbs and nothing in the house has changed, start with a simple test. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the water meter's leak indicator. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere. Our leak detection service narrows down where, so repairs happen in one spot instead of several guesses. If a supply line has failed, burst pipe repair and water line repair cover the fix.

Before We Arrive: A Drain Backing Up In An Older Home

  • Stop running water in the house. Every fixture you use sends more water toward the blockage.
  • Skip chemical drain openers if you plan to have the line inspected, because they make the pipe harder and less safe to work on.
  • If you know where the sewer cleanout is, clear the area around it so we can reach it quickly.
  • Call us and tell us which fixtures are affected. One slow drain and every drain backing up point to different problems.
