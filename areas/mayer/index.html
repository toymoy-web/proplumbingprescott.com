A Small Highway 69 Community With A Wide Service Footprint.

Mayer is a census-designated place along Highway 69, southeast of Dewey-Humboldt and roughly 25 miles from Prescott. The town itself counted 1,558 residents in 2020, but ZIP code 86333 covers a far larger rural area of hundreds of square miles with several thousand more people. Elevation is around 4,400 feet. Because the ZIP reaches so far, an address in Mayer can be a town-center building or a ranch property miles from the highway.

Older Buildings And Mixed Plumbing.

Mayer's town center includes three sites on the National Register of Historic Places: the Mayer Apartments, the Mayer Business Block, and the Mayer Red Brick Schoolhouse. That tells you the age of the building stock. Older structures often combine original pipe with later repairs, so we identify the pipe material and the shutoff locations before quoting a job. Where supply lines are failing in several places, our repiping page explains when replacement beats patching.

District Water Versus Private Wells.

Part of the community is served by the Mayer Domestic Water Improvement District, which became a public improvement district in 1993 after years as a privately owned system. Properties outside its lines depend on other sources, including private wells. The difference matters in an emergency. If the whole street has lost water, the issue may be the district. If only your house is dry, the problem is on your side of the meter, and that is where we can help.

Spring Valley And The Septic Question.

Spring Valley, the neighboring community along the same stretch of Highway 69, is home to Mayer High School and is part of the Mayer ZIP. Many properties in Mayer and Spring Valley use septic tanks rather than a sewer. Slow drains, gurgling, and soggy ground near the tank are the usual warning signs. We check the tank side as well as the pipes, and our septic tank pumping page covers routine care.

Monsoon Washes And Flash Flood Season.

Big Bug Wash runs through the area, and monsoon storms can move water fast. In 2022, storm damage to a bank near the wash threatened a main line serving the Mayer water district, and residents were asked to store emergency water. Low yards and crawlspaces near washes are where a failed sump pump hurts most, so we recommend testing pumps before monsoon season starts each year.

Emergency Dispatch Along Highway 69.

Mayer is in our outer service ring, past Prescott Valley and Dewey-Humboldt. Our emergency line answers around the clock, and a technician drives down Highway 69 as soon as dispatch confirms the job. We tell you plainly how long the drive will take rather than promising a time we cannot meet. If a crew is already working in Dewey-Humboldt, we say so, because it can shorten the wait.

What We Fix Most Often In Mayer.

Water heaters are a frequent call, and at roughly 4,400 feet any gas unit still needs altitude derating because Mayer sits well above the 2,000-foot threshold. Our water heater repair page explains what we check. Well pumps, septic backups, and aging water lines make up most of the rest, which is a different mix than the newer subdivisions in Prescott Valley.

How Mayer Fits With Dewey-Humboldt And Prescott Valley.

Mayer is the next community down the Highway 69 corridor from Dewey and Humboldt, whose ZIP codes are 86327 and 86329. Our Dewey-Humboldt and Prescott Valley pages cover the stretch between here and Prescott. Homes along that whole corridor share the same mix of septic, well, and older plumbing concerns.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak.

  • Shut off the main water valve at the house first, not just the fixture nearest the leak.
  • Turn off power at the breaker to any room with standing water if it is safe to reach.
  • Move people and pets away from the wet area and stay out of any water near outlets.
  • Call our 24/7 line, tell us you are in Mayer or Spring Valley, and we will confirm an arrival window.
