Replacement is a bigger job than a spot repair or trenchless lining, and it is the right call specifically when the line's problems are structural and widespread rather than isolated to one defect a camera can point to and fix locally.

When A Line Needs Full Replacement, Not Repair.

A bellied section, where the pipe has settled and lost its proper downward slope, cannot be corrected by lining or a spot patch, since the underlying problem is the pipe's position and grade, not a hole or crack in it. The only real fix is removing and replacing that section with correct grading restored.

A clay or cast iron lateral with root intrusion at many joints along its full length is common in Prescott's oldest neighborhoods. It sometimes passes the point where trenchless lining remains cost-effective compared to full replacement, especially once the pipe's structural integrity is compromised at multiple points rather than one or two.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • Repeated sewer backups despite multiple prior repairs.
  • A camera inspection showing a collapsed or severely bellied section.
  • Root intrusion confirmed at multiple points along the line's length.
  • A sinkhole or significant settling in the yard along the line's path.
  • A line so old and deteriorated that spot repairs no longer hold.

Confirming Replacement Is Necessary

A full camera inspection covering the entire line's length, not just the area where symptoms show up, confirms whether the problem is truly isolated enough for a smaller repair or genuinely widespread enough to justify full replacement. We do not recommend replacement without this complete picture, since it is the more expensive option and deserves real justification.

Trenchless Pipe Bursting Versus Open Trench

Trenchless pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old line's path while breaking apart the old pipe as it goes, avoiding a continuous open trench across the yard in many cases. Open trench replacement is more invasive but sometimes necessary when the line's path runs under a structure, a driveway, or where pipe bursting is not a safe option given what is nearby.

Sewer lateral replacement is generally excluded from standard homeowner's policies unless a specific sewer line endorsement was added, since gradual pipe failure is treated as a maintenance issue rather than a sudden covered event.

Trenchless Bursting Vs. Open Trench

Trenchless Pipe Bursting

  • Minimal trenching across the yard.
  • Faster restoration of landscaping.
  • Not always possible near certain obstacles.
  • Often the preferred method when conditions allow.

Open Trench Replacement

  • Works in situations trenchless cannot address.
  • More disruptive to landscaping during the work.
  • Sometimes necessary near structures or tight utility corridors.
  • Full visual access to the entire line during replacement.
