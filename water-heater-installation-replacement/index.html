Replacement is a different job from repair, and it is worth doing right the first time. That means confirming the correct tank size for the household, the correct gas line and venting for an altitude-derated burner, and code items like an expansion tank that a repair visit would never touch.

Why A New Installation Needs More Than A Swap

Modern plumbing code in most jurisdictions, including here, requires a thermal expansion tank on a closed water system, one with a check valve or pressure-reducing valve at the meter. Many older Prescott homes were plumbed before this requirement existed, so a straight swap of an old unit for a new one can leave a home out of code compliance even though the water heater itself works fine.

Sizing also matters more than it might seem. A tank sized for a two-person household installed in a home that has since grown to a family of five runs out of hot water constantly, not because anything is broken, but because the unit was never right for the household's actual demand. We size a replacement to current household needs, not just a like-for-like swap of whatever was there before.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • Your existing water heater is past 8 to 12 years old.
  • You are remodeling and want to right-size for a growing household.
  • Your current unit was never altitude-derated and underperforms.
  • You are converting from tank to tankless or switching fuel types.
  • A home inspection flagged a missing expansion tank or code issue.

What We Check Before Recommending A Unit

We look at household size and peak hot water demand, existing gas line capacity and venting, available install space, and whether the existing water line and electrical connections meet current code. This tells us whether a straightforward like-for-like replacement works or whether the job needs additional code-related work done at the same time.

Tank Size And Fuel Type Options

Standard tank sizes run from 40 to 50 gallons for most households, with larger units available for bigger homes or higher-demand fixtures. Gas units cost less to operate in most cases but need correct altitude-derated sizing; electric units skip that consideration entirely. We walk through the real trade-offs for your specific household rather than defaulting to whatever unit happens to be in stock.

Like-For-Like Swap Vs. Full Code-Compliant Replacement

Straight Swap

  • Lower upfront cost.
  • May leave a missing expansion tank unaddressed.
  • Does not correct an undersized original install.
  • Fastest option for an already-compliant system.

Full Compliant Replacement

  • Brings the installation up to current code.
  • Correctly sized for actual household demand.
  • Altitude-derated from day one.
  • Recommended whenever the old install predates current code.

Basic Gas Safety Steps For Any New Installation.

  • If you ever smell gas near a water heater, leave the area and shut off the gas supply at the meter or appliance valve if it is safe to do so.
  • Call your gas utility's emergency line before attempting to relight anything yourself.
  • Never operate a gas water heater in a space with blocked or damaged venting.
  • A carbon monoxide detector near any gas appliance is a genuinely inexpensive safeguard worth having.
