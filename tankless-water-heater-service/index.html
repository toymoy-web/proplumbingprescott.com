A tankless unit heats water on demand rather than storing it, using a high-output burner or heating element. That higher output makes correct sizing, venting, and combustion air supply more important than on a standard tank unit, especially at Prescott's elevation.

Why Tankless Units Need Specific Local Attention

A tankless burner's higher output is more sensitive to combustion air and elevation than a standard tank burner, so venting design and altitude-derated sizing both need to be correct from installation. A unit installed by a technician unfamiliar with Prescott's roughly 5,300-foot elevation can underperform even though nothing is mechanically wrong with it.

Hard water is the other major factor. A tankless unit's heat exchanger has much narrower internal passages than a tank's storage volume, so mineral scale restricts flow faster and more noticeably here than it would in a soft-water market. Regular descaling is not optional maintenance for a tankless unit in this area the way it might be treated elsewhere.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • Reduced hot water flow rate compared to when the unit was new.
  • An error code displayed on the unit's control panel.
  • The unit takes longer to deliver hot water than it used to.
  • A rattling or unusual sound during operation.
  • The pilot or ignition fails intermittently.

How We Diagnose A Tankless Problem

We pull the unit's stored fault code first, which usually points directly at the affected component. From there we check the flow sensor and heat exchanger for scale restriction, confirm venting and combustion air are unobstructed, and verify the burner is still correctly sized for elevation if the installation history is unclear.

Descaling Versus Repair

A unit with reduced flow and no error code is often simply due for a descaling service, which flushes mineral buildup from the heat exchanger and restores normal performance. A unit throwing a specific fault code usually points to a failed component, such as a flow sensor or ignition module, that needs actual replacement rather than descaling alone.

Regular Descaling Vs. Skipping Maintenance

Skipping Descaling

  • No upfront maintenance cost.
  • Flow rate degrades gradually over time.
  • Higher risk of a fault-code failure requiring repair.
  • Shortens the unit's overall working life.

Annual Descaling

  • Maintains consistent flow and output.
  • Lower risk of a sudden component failure.
  • Extends the unit toward its full 15 to 20 year lifespan.
  • Recommended yearly in this hard water area.

Basic Gas Safety Steps For Tankless Units.

  • If you smell gas near the unit, leave the area and shut off the gas supply if it is safe to do so, then call your gas utility's emergency line.
  • Never attempt to relight or reset a unit that shut down for a suspected gas issue until a professional confirms it is safe.
  • Keep the exterior vent termination clear of snow, debris, or anything that could restrict airflow.
  • A carbon monoxide detector near the unit is a simple, worthwhile safeguard.
