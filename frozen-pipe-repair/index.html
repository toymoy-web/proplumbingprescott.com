Frozen Pipe Repair in Prescott, AZ

A frozen supply line, whether it has already split or is simply blocking water flow, is a real winter risk at Prescott's elevation. We thaw and repair frozen lines and retrofit insulation to prevent it happening again.

Prescott's roughly 5,300-foot elevation means genuinely cold winter nights that lower-desert Arizona cities rarely experience, and an exposed supply line in a crawlspace, garage, or exterior wall is a real candidate for freezing during a hard cold snap.

Why This Happens Here

Water expands as it freezes, and that expansion inside a confined pipe generates enough pressure to split copper, PEX, or galvanized pipe from the inside. This most often happens in unconditioned spaces, crawlspaces, garages, and attics, where a supply line was never insulated because freeze was assumed to be a non-issue in Arizona generally.

Older homes near downtown Prescott, built before insulation standards accounted for real freeze risk at this elevation, are particularly exposed. A single hard cold snap can freeze an exposed line that has never had a problem in previous, milder winters.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • No water, or a noticeably reduced flow, from a specific fixture during a cold snap.
  • Visible frost or ice on an exposed exterior or crawlspace pipe.
  • A bulging section of pipe that has not yet split.
  • Water actively leaking or spraying, indicating the pipe has already split.
  • Unusually cold air noticeable near a wall where a pipe runs.

Safely Thawing A Frozen Line

We locate the frozen section and apply controlled heat, using methods appropriate to the pipe material and location, rather than an open flame which risks igniting nearby materials or damaging the pipe further. If the pipe has already split, thawing confirms the exact repair location once the water and any ice inside have cleared.

Thawing, Repair, And Prevention Together

A frozen but intact line typically just needs safe thawing and, ideally, insulation added afterward to prevent a repeat freeze on the next cold night. A line that has already split needs the damaged section repaired in addition to thawing, and we address both in the same visit whenever possible rather than requiring a second appointment.

Thawing Alone Vs. Thawing Plus Insulation

Thaw Only

  • Lower immediate cost.
  • Restores water flow right away.
  • Same spot remains vulnerable to the next cold snap.
  • Reasonable if this was a one-time unusual cold event.

Thaw Plus Insulation Retrofit

  • Addresses the immediate problem and the underlying cause.
  • Meaningfully reduces risk of a repeat freeze at the same spot.
  • Slightly higher cost for the added insulation work.
  • Recommended for any line that has frozen before.

What Not To Do When Thawing A Pipe.

  • Never use an open flame, such as a propane torch, to thaw a pipe. This is a real fire risk and can also damage the pipe further.
  • Shut off the water supply to the affected line first if you suspect it may have already split, before attempting any thawing.
  • If water and electrical outlets or wiring are near each other in the affected area, shut off power to that area at the breaker panel if it is safe to reach.
  • A hair dryer or a space heater kept at a safe distance from combustible material are reasonable, safer thawing methods for an accessible section.
