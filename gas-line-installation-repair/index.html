Gas line work is one area where cutting corners genuinely is not an option. Every installation and repair gets pressure tested before we consider the job complete, and every new appliance hookup is sized correctly for that specific appliance's demand.

Why Older Gas Lines Need Careful Evaluation

Black iron gas pipe common in homes built before the 1970s can corrode at threaded joints over decades, similar to how galvanized water supply lines corrode. A gas line that has never been inspected since the home was built is a reasonable thing to have checked, particularly before adding a new gas appliance that increases demand on the existing line.

Adding a new gas appliance, such as a range, a fireplace insert, or a tankless water heater, sometimes requires upsizing the existing line rather than simply tapping into it. The added demand can exceed what the current pipe diameter can safely deliver to every appliance on the line at once.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • A sulfur or rotten-egg smell near an appliance or gas meter.
  • A hissing sound near a gas line or fitting.
  • A gas appliance that burns with a yellow flame instead of blue.
  • Dead or discolored vegetation near an underground gas line.
  • Unusually high gas bills with no change in appliance use.

How We Test A Gas Line

We pressure test the line to confirm it holds steady with no drop over time, which reveals even a small leak that might not be detectable by smell alone. Every new installation and every repair gets this same test before we consider the job complete, not just a visual inspection.

Repair Versus Line Replacement

A single failed fitting or a short damaged section is usually a straightforward repair. A line with corrosion at multiple points, or one that is undersized for a planned new appliance, is a better candidate for a longer section replacement or resizing rather than patching one spot and running into the same problem again soon.

Spot Repair Vs. Line Replacement

Single Fitting Repair

  • Lower cost for an isolated failure.
  • Faster to complete.
  • Does not address other aging sections of the same line.
  • Appropriate for a line otherwise in good condition.

Section Replacement Or Resizing

  • Addresses corrosion along a longer run, not just one point.
  • Necessary when adding a high-demand new appliance.
  • Higher upfront cost than a single repair.
  • Recommended for older black iron lines with multiple issues.

If You Smell Gas.

  • Leave the area immediately and do not operate any light switch, phone, or appliance inside the space.
  • Do not attempt to locate the leak yourself or turn any valves on or off.
  • Call your gas utility's emergency line from a safe distance, then call a licensed plumber.
  • Do not return to the area until the utility or a professional confirms it is safe.
