A slab leak happens when a supply line running underneath a concrete foundation develops a pinhole leak, usually in copper pipe, and the escaping water has nowhere to go but up through or along the slab. The sooner it is found, the smaller the repair.

Why Slab Leaks Show Up In Prescott's Newer Construction

Slab-on-grade construction is common in the newer subdivisions toward Prescott Valley and the east side of town, where copper supply lines run directly beneath the concrete foundation rather than through an accessible crawlspace. Copper pipe under a slab can develop a pinhole leak from a combination of water chemistry, soil conditions, and simple age, even in a house that is only fifteen or twenty years old.

Once a pinhole leak starts, water pressure pushes it outward along the path of least resistance, which is often up through the slab itself or along a plumbing chase. That is why a slab leak often shows up as a warm spot on tile or hardwood flooring well before any visible water appears, especially when the leak is on the hot water line.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • A warm or hot spot on the floor with no obvious source.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture in the house is off.
  • A water bill spike with no change in household water use.
  • Mildew smell or damp carpet near an interior wall with no visible leak.
  • Low water pressure at a fixture with no other explanation.

Pinpointing The Leak Under The Slab

We use acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging together to narrow down the leak's location before any cutting starts. Acoustic equipment picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure through the concrete, while thermal imaging can reveal the heat signature of a hot water line leak. Combining both narrows the repair to a small, targeted area instead of an exploratory search.

Spot Repair Vs. Rerouting The Line

Once the leak is pinpointed, a small, targeted cut directly over the leak location often allows for a direct repair with minimal disruption to flooring. If the affected line shows signs of age-related pitting beyond the single leak point, rerouting that section through an accessible wall or ceiling space above the slab avoids repeated future slab cuts on the same line.

Spot Repair vs. Full Reroute

Spot Repair Over The Leak

  • Smaller, more contained cut into the slab or flooring.
  • Lower upfront cost for a single, isolated leak.
  • Does not address other aging sections of the same line.
  • Best for a young line with one clear failure point.

Reroute Above The Slab

  • Moves the line out of the slab entirely going forward.
  • Removes the risk of a second slab leak on the same run.
  • Higher upfront cost than a single spot repair.
  • Recommended after a second slab leak on the same line.
