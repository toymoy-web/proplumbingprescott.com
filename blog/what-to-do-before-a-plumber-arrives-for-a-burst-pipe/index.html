What To Do Before A Plumber Arrives For A Burst Pipe

By the Prescott Pro Plumbing team · 2026-09-17.

The minutes before a plumber arrives matter more than people realize when a pipe has burst. What you do in that window has more effect on the final damage than how quickly we can get to your door.

Shut Off The Main Water Valve First.

Find and shut off the home's main water shutoff valve immediately, not just the valve nearest the leak, since a burst anywhere in the system stays pressurized until the main supply is closed. In most Prescott-area homes, this valve sits near the water meter at the street or where the main line enters the house, often in a crawlspace, garage, or a utility closet. If you do not already know where yours is, finding it now, before an actual emergency happens, is worth the five minutes it takes.

Shut Off Electricity To Any Affected Area.

If water is near any electrical outlets, switches, or fixtures, shut off power to that area at the breaker panel if it is safe to reach without walking through standing water. Water and electricity are a genuine safety hazard together, and this step takes priority over anything related to the plumbing itself if there is any doubt about safety at all in the moment.

Contain What You Can Without Risk.

Towels, buckets, or a wet-dry vacuum can limit how far water spreads while waiting for help to arrive, but do not delay shutting off the main valve in order to start cleanup first. Stopping the source of water always takes priority over managing water that has already escaped, since every additional minute the main stays open adds more water to clean up later.

Move Valuables And Furniture Away From Water.

If it is safe and quick to do, move furniture, electronics, and valuables away from spreading water, particularly anything on the floor in the affected room. This is a lower priority than the water and electrical safety steps above, but it is worth doing if there is a genuinely safe moment to do it before the plumber actually arrives at the door.

Photograph The Damage Before Cleanup Begins.

Take photos or video of the visible damage before extensive cleanup starts, since this documentation is often the first thing an insurance adjuster asks for if you plan to file a claim for water damage. This takes only a minute and can meaningfully simplify a claims process that would otherwise rely on memory and description alone.

Staying Calm Matters More Than It Sounds.

A burst pipe looks and sounds worse in the first minute than the actual damage usually turns out to be once the main is shut off and the situation is properly assessed. Taking thirty seconds to find the main valve calmly, rather than panicking and searching frantically, actually gets the water stopped faster in practice than rushing does, and it keeps everyone in the house safer in the process too.

What Not To Do.

Do not attempt to patch a burst pipe yourself with tape or a clamp as anything more than a very temporary measure, since a poor patch under full water pressure can fail suddenly and worse than the original break. Do not run additional water anywhere in the house while waiting for the main valve to be addressed, and do not ignore an electrical hazard near standing water in order to save a few minutes on cleanup.

What Happens When We Arrive.

We confirm the main is properly shut off, locate and isolate the specific damaged section, and assess whether the failure is an isolated event or a sign of broader issues with that supply line, such as widespread galvanized corrosion. From there we explain what we found and give a firm price before starting the actual repair, the same standard we hold to on every call regardless of how urgent it is.

Preventing The Next Burst Pipe.

Once the immediate emergency is resolved, it is worth asking whether the burst was an isolated event or a sign of a broader problem, particularly on an older galvanized system where one failure often signals others are not far behind. We will tell you honestly which situation applies to your home rather than simply fixing the visible break and moving on without mentioning a pattern we genuinely noticed.

Keeping Emergency Information Accessible.

Knowing your main shutoff valve location before an emergency happens, rather than searching for it during one, is the single most useful piece of preparation any homeowner can do. Consider labeling the valve clearly and making sure everyone in the household knows where it is and how to operate it, since a burst pipe rarely waits for the one person who normally handles home maintenance to be present.

Why Winter Nights Deserve Extra Caution Here.

Given Prescott's real freeze risk at elevation, a burst pipe emergency is more likely to happen overnight during a cold snap than at a convenient daytime hour. That is exactly why our emergency line answers 24 hours a day rather than routing after-hours calls to voicemail. Knowing help is available at 2 a.m. as readily as at 2 p.m. matters when a genuine emergency does not wait for business hours.

A Short Recap Worth Keeping Somewhere Handy.

Shut off the main water valve first, not just the nearest fixture. Cut power to any area near standing water if it is safe to reach the breaker panel. Contain what water you safely can without delaying the first step, move valuables away from the affected area, and photograph the damage before cleanup begins in earnest. These five steps, done roughly in this order, make a genuine difference in how much damage a burst pipe ultimately causes, and they take less time to run through than it takes to read this sentence twice.
