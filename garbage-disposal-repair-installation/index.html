A disposal problem ranges from a simple jam that resets in minutes to a unit that has failed entirely and needs replacement. Knowing which one you have before spending money on the wrong fix saves both time and cost.

Why Disposals Jam Or Fail

A jam is usually mechanical: something too hard or fibrous, like a fruit pit, bone fragment, or celery string, has lodged against the impeller plate. This is the most common disposal call and often resolves with the reset button and manual clearing rather than a repair.

A leak, by contrast, usually points to a failed seal somewhere in the unit, either where it connects to the sink drain or internally around the motor housing. Once a disposal is leaking rather than just jamming, repair options narrow and replacement often becomes the more sensible choice given the unit's age at that point.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • The disposal hums but does not spin when turned on.
  • Water leaking from underneath the unit.
  • A burning smell when the disposal runs.
  • Unusually loud grinding or rattling during operation.
  • The unit will not turn on at all, even after resetting.

What We Check Before Recommending A Fix

A humming unit that will not spin usually means a jam we can clear manually. No power at all points toward an electrical issue, either the unit's internal switch or the circuit itself. A leak gets traced to its specific source, whether that is the sink flange, the discharge connection, or the unit's internal housing.

Reset And Repair Versus Replacement

A simple jam is often resolved on the spot with the reset button and manual clearing, at minimal cost. A unit with a failed internal seal or motor is usually not worth repairing given how affordable a new disposal is relative to a specialized internal repair, so replacement is the more common recommendation once a unit is actually leaking or has lost power entirely.

Reset And Repair Vs. Full Replacement

Reset Or Repair

  • Lowest cost option for a simple jam.
  • Keeps the existing unit in service.
  • Not viable once a unit is actually leaking.
  • Best for an otherwise healthy, recently jammed unit.

Full Replacement

  • Necessary once internal seals or the motor have failed.
  • Often more cost-effective than a specialized internal repair.
  • Opportunity to upgrade horsepower if needed.
  • Recommended for any unit past 8 to 12 years old.
