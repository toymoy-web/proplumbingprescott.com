A dripping faucet is more than a nuisance sound. Left long enough, it wastes real water and can lead to mineral staining or cabinet water damage underneath. Most faucet issues are quick, inexpensive fixes once diagnosed correctly.

Why Faucets Fail Faster In Hard Water Areas

Internal faucet components, particularly cartridges, washers, and O-rings, wear out faster in hard water areas like Prescott than they would in a soft-water market. Mineral deposits build up on these small moving parts, preventing a clean seal and causing the steady drip that brings most faucet repair calls.

Under-sink connections, supply lines, and P-traps fail for a different reason: age and vibration loosening a fitting over years of normal use. A slow leak under a sink often goes unnoticed until cabinet damage or a musty smell finally draws attention to it.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • A faucet that drips steadily even when fully shut off.
  • Water pooling under the sink inside the cabinet.
  • Reduced water flow or a wobbly, loose faucet handle.
  • Mineral staining building up around the faucet base.
  • A sink that drains slowly, suggesting a P-trap or connection issue.

What We Check On A Faucet Call

We check the cartridge or valve mechanism first, since this is the most common source of a persistent drip, followed by supply line connections and the P-trap if there is any sign of moisture under the sink. A wobbly handle usually points to a loose mounting nut rather than anything more involved.

Repair Versus Full Fixture Replacement

A worn cartridge or a loose connection is usually a straightforward, inexpensive repair on an otherwise sound fixture. A faucet that has needed more than one repair, or one that is simply outdated and no longer matches a remodeled kitchen or bathroom, is often better replaced outright rather than continuing to patch an aging fixture.

Repair Vs. Replace An Aging Faucet

Repair The Existing Faucet

  • Lower cost for a straightforward part replacement.
  • Keeps a otherwise sound fixture in service.
  • Parts may be harder to find on older models.
  • Does not update the fixture's look or features.

Replace With A New Fixture

  • Fresh look to match a remodeled space.
  • Modern features like pull-down sprayers or touchless operation.
  • Higher upfront cost than a single repair.
  • Recommended once a faucet needs its second or third repair.
