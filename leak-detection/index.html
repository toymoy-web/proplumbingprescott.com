Most leak detection calls start the same way: the homeowner knows something is wrong but has no idea where. We confirm there actually is a leak first, using the water meter itself as a simple test, before bringing in acoustic and thermal equipment to pinpoint the exact location.

Where Hidden Leaks Actually Come From In Prescott

Slab leaks are a real concern in the newer construction toward Prescott Valley and the east side of town, where homes are built on concrete slab foundations with supply lines running underneath. A pinhole leak in a copper line under the slab can run for months before it surfaces as a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill.

In the older neighborhoods near downtown, wall leaks are more common, often from a corroded joint in an original galvanized line or a fitting that has slowly loosened over decades. These leaks can run inside a wall cavity for a long time without an obvious sign beyond a musty smell or a slightly higher bill each month.

Signs You Need This Service.

  • A water bill noticeably higher than the same month last year with no change in usage.
  • The water meter's leak indicator still turning with every fixture in the house off.
  • A warm spot on the floor, often a sign of a hot water slab leak.
  • A musty smell or soft spot in flooring near an exterior wall.
  • The sound of running water when nothing is turned on.

How Electronic Leak Detection Works

We start by confirming a real leak exists using the water meter test: shut off every fixture and check whether the meter's leak indicator still moves. From there, acoustic listening equipment traces the sound of water escaping under pressure, and thermal imaging can reveal a temperature difference where a hot water line leak has warmed the slab or wall around it. This finds the leak without cutting open walls or flooring on a guess.

Repair Options Once The Leak Is Found

A pinpointed slab leak can sometimes be repaired with a small, targeted cut directly over the leak rather than tearing up an entire room. A wall leak at a single fitting is often a simple point repair. If a line has multiple failure points from age rather than one isolated leak, rerouting that section above the slab or through an accessible wall cavity is sometimes more cost-effective than repeated spot repairs.
